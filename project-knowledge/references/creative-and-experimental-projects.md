# Knowledge in Creative and Experimental Work

Read this reference when project knowledge must serve creative or experimental work whose success depends on experience, taste, prototype learning, or another result that cannot be judged by one reliable automated oracle. It extends the core model with the relationships and evidence distinctive to that work.

For the underlying research on multimodal game development and human creative evaluation, and for the authoritative MDA source, see the selectively loaded [research index](research/README.md).

## Start from the intended experience

Creative work often begins with an intended experience: a mood, tension, rhythm, visual direction, sense of agency, or kind of curiosity. The project implements mechanisms and systems that produce actual dynamics, and people encounter those dynamics as an experience. Intent, mechanism, actual dynamics, and experience are related but not identical.

Use this as an analysis lens when it changes what the project should do next:

```text
experience intent → mechanisms and systems → actual dynamics → human experience
```

MDA (Mechanics, Dynamics, Aesthetics) can help name these relationships. Preserve them in whatever existing vision, design, prototype, or production carriers readers can actually find and use. Keep a desired feeling distinct from a claim about what the current implementation produces.

When the relationship is uncertain, record the hypothesis and link to the playable prototype, scene, asset, parameter set, or recording that lets a collaborator inspect it. Keep observed behavior separate from the intended experience and from a person's interpretation of that behavior.

## Distinguish delivery from learning

Not every milestone exists to finish production. A **delivery milestone** produces a known result, such as a working feature, scene, build, or integration. A **learning milestone** reduces an important uncertainty, such as whether a mechanic creates the desired rhythm or whether an interaction is worth keeping.

A prototype may succeed by answering a question rather than by becoming production-ready. Its outcome might be `KEEP`, `MODIFY`, or `KILL`, with the reasoning and evidence recorded at the appropriate level of durability. Put both milestone kinds in the project's actual plan or experiment record as the existing structure warrants.

Make the question, observable signal, artifact, and resulting decision easy to find. If the result changes a durable goal, design, decision, or direction, update that authoritative project knowledge instead of leaving the lesson in a temporary session note.

## Use the best carrier and connection

Prose cannot precisely communicate every quality of an experience. Depending on the question, use the most faithful interactive, visual, audio, measured, or participant-carried artifact rather than forcing the experience into prose.

Connect these artifacts from the relevant durable source and state what the reader should inspect. Keep enough prose to explain purpose, relationship, provenance, and interpretation, but do not flatten a sensory or interactive result into an overconfident summary.

## Separate kinds of evidence

Creative projects can need several kinds of evidence, each answering a different question:

- **Technical evidence** shows that code, assets, scenes, builds, or integrations behave as claimed.
- **Behavioral evidence** shows what the system does across inputs, states, or observed runs, including telemetry and structured playtest observations.
- **Human experiential judgment** addresses whether people understand, enjoy, feel, notice, or value the result, and whether it matches the intended direction.

An automated check can support technical or behavioral claims without proving that an experience is compelling. An Agent can analyze recordings, propose evaluation criteria, simulate users, or find reachable-state problems. Identify those outputs as Agent analysis, and label their evidence, source, and limits.

## Keep authority visible

When taste, aesthetic direction, conceptual risk, or the final experience is at issue, identify who or what has authority to decide. Treat real user or human stakeholder feedback as evidence distinct from Agent analysis, and preserve disagreements or open questions when they affect direction.

## Let these differences change knowledge jobs

For a creative or experimental project, durable knowledge may need to preserve:

- the intended experience and the audience or context for it;
- the system or mechanism believed to support that intent;
- the question a prototype or experiment is meant to answer;
- the artifact and evidence through which readers can inspect the result;
- the learning, decision, or next direction that follows.

These responsibilities may fit the project's existing vision, design, decision, plan, experiment, or playtest carriers. Let actual readers and change cycles determine whether responsibilities stay together or separate, and choose the smallest arrangement that gives future readers a reliable path from intent to artifact to learning.
