# Collaboration

## Writing shared records

Use the project's collaboration language for issue and PR titles, bodies, and comments. Title them by the problem, requested outcome, or proposed change. Follow explicit project title formats where present, and use existing labels for classification.

Follow the applicable artifact template. Give readers the background and accessible references needed to understand the subject and next action. Distinguish facts, hypotheses, proposals, and decisions. Explain review concerns and whether they block acceptance. Scale detail and structure to the purpose.

## Maintaining records

<Where available, link the existing work-tracker and domain-document rules.>

Use the record designated by the chosen workflow as the current authority. It may be a body, a document, or a designated comment. Discuss work at the relevant record; link related discussions rather than copying them.

Determine maintenance responsibility from the current task, handoff, or workflow. Otherwise, the creator maintains the body and others propose changes in comments. With shared accounts, establish responsibility from task context rather than the account name. Read the latest content before editing.

Maintain records within the agreed requirements. Carry out authorized changes to behavior, acceptance criteria, or dependencies; refer unresolved changes to the responsible decision-maker. Update affected active records and link the decision. Preserve completed work's original scope when recording later requirements.

Keep current summaries current. Correct substantive errors in historical discussion with a linked follow-up; fix minor wording or formatting in place.

At handoff, record the current result, remaining work, evidence, and next responsibility. Share necessary private environment details through an appropriate private channel. Record established reusable lessons in the relevant existing conventions.

## Sharing information

Apply the destination's agreed sharing scope to files, commit messages, discussions, and attachments, including private remotes. Publish only what the intended readers need.

Replace personal machine paths and private conversation references with repository-relative paths or neutral descriptions. Extract and sanitize relevant log, screenshot, and sample content. Use placeholders or access instructions for credentials, never live secrets. Preserve intended public attribution.

Reuse established sharing decisions. If necessary material has an unclear sharing boundary, clarify that material before publishing; use a sanitized explanation when sufficient.
